Roof Leak Water Damage Help in Jeffersonville

The spot on your ceiling is seldom directly below the hole in the roof. Water enters at a raised shingle, a split in the flashing or a worn skylight seal, follows the rafters and decking downhill, and finally lands on the insulation. Often the attic has been damp for some time before any brown mark reaches the room below. Locating all of the moisture and drying it is our job.

We are a water damage crew, not a roofing company. We work on the interior, which means removing wet insulation, drying drywall and framing, dealing with mold and rebuilding the ceiling. When the roof is still letting water through, a temporary tarp is something we can install, and we will coordinate with your roofer so the repair comes before the ceiling is closed.

Do This First

Your First Steps

  1. Cut Power to Wet FixturesTurn off the breaker for any light, fan or outlet that has water running through or near it, then keep your hands off it.
  2. Catch the DripsFurniture, electronics and rugs should be moved aside, with buckets placed wherever water is falling. Protect the floor with plastic sheeting or old towels.
  3. Drain a Bulging CeilingWhen water pools in a bubble overhead, pierce the low point with a screwdriver while standing clear, and let it run into a bucket.
  4. Leave the Roof AloneClimbing on slick shingles or crawling around a dark attic is not worth the risk. Note from inside where the water is coming in and leave the rest to professionals.
  5. Document What You SeeUse your phone to record the damaged ceiling, attic and belongings first, hold off on throwing things out until that is done, and call us to get the drying started.
Will Insurance Pay?

Does Insurance Cover It?

Interior damage from a sudden, accidental roof leak, like one caused by a fallen limb or wind-torn shingles, is often covered under a homeowners policy. Insurers generally turn down claims tied to wear, age or lack of upkeep, and they may handle the roof repair on different terms than the inside damage. Photos and moisture readings from us go to the adjuster, though only your policy decides coverage, and your agent can explain it.

Why It Happens

What Causes It

  • Aging, cracked seals around a skylight frame that no longer keep water out.
  • Nails that have backed out of the decking and left small holes through the shingles.
  • Storm impact, such as hail or a tree branch, breaking through the shingles.
  • Flashing that has rusted, lifted or pulled away around chimneys, vents and walls.
  • A vent pipe boot whose rubber has split from long sun exposure.
What Is at Risk

Where the Damage Spreads

Ceiling Fixtures and Wiring

Because fixtures are holes in the drywall, a leak often shows up at a light or fan first. Moisture in a fixture can cause a short and later corrosion, which is why it needs inspection before use.

Hidden Attic Mold

Mold does well in an attic that is warm, dark and full of damp wood and drywall paper. Within 24 to 48 hours it can start growing, then move across the bottom of the roof deck.

Roof Framing

Rafters, trusses and decking that get wet again and again begin to darken, swell and soften. Decay here means less strength in what carries the roof.

Attic Insulation

For days after a leak, matted insulation sits wet on top of the ceiling and no longer insulates. In some cases fiberglass batts dry out and stay. Cellulose that is saturated generally comes out.

How We Fix It

How We Handle Roof Leak Water Damage

1

Trace the Leak Path

Moisture meters and a thermal camera let us follow the rain through the attic, the ceilings and the walls, so spots that look dry from underneath are not missed.

2

Cover and Protect

When rain can still get in, a temporary tarp from us or a call to your roofer comes first. Floors and belongings below the wet ceiling are covered as well.

3

Tear Out and Dry

We pull out insulation and drywall that cannot be saved, then run air movers and dehumidifiers in the attic and the rooms below, checking moisture readings until the framing is dry.

4

Treat and Rebuild

An antimicrobial product goes on any open wood that needs it. After the roofer has finished, new insulation goes in, fresh drywall is hung and finished, and the ceiling is primed and painted to match.
