Attic Water Damage in Jeffersonville

Attic water damage comes from two directions. Rain gets in from above through worn shingles, failed flashing, or the seals around vents and chimneys. Moisture also rises from the house below and condenses on cold roof sheathing when the attic is not ventilated well. In both cases, almost nobody climbs up to check. In Jeffersonville, we inspect the attic, track down the source, and get it dry.

Most attic jobs center on wet insulation. Heating and cooling bills go up because cellulose and fiberglass alike give up R-value when wet. Even worse, it keeps water pressed against the framing and the ceiling drywall for weeks. Our process is to pull the saturated insulation, dry the wood using dehumidifiers and air movers, and treat mold found on rafters or sheathing.

Warning Signs

  • A musty smell in the attic or in the upstairs closets
  • After rain, a stain or brown ring on a top floor ceiling that looks worse
  • Bubbling or peeling ceiling paint in a room on the top floor
  • Rusty nail tips poking through the sheathing, sometimes with water beads on them
  • Streaks or dark staining on the roof deck's underside and on the rafters
  • A bath fan or ceiling light fixture with drips or a stain around it
Why Speed Matters

The Damage Timeline

First storm

Insulation Soaks It Up

Rain that makes it through the roof falls into the insulation and is absorbed. Because the rooms below show nothing, the leak is missed and no roof repair happens.

Hours 24 to 48

Mold Can Start

In a still, warm attic, paper facing and wood that stay damp give mold ideal conditions. Growth can begin on the sheathing and on the back of the ceiling drywall.

Weeks later

Ceiling Stains Appear

Repeated wetting saturates the insulation, and water gets down to the ceiling. Brown rings appear in the room below, and the drywall may start to sag.

After months

Rot Sets In

The sheathing comes apart in layers and no longer grips the roofing nails. Mold spreads across large sections of the roof deck, and the rafters remain damp enough to decay.

What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

Insulation That Stops Working

Insulation that is damp and compressed barely insulates, and drying may not bring it back. The rooms beneath that section get colder or hotter, and you pay more for energy.

Wet Wiring

An attic contains recessed light cans, junction boxes and runs of cable. Water in or on them is a shock and fire risk, so stay out until the power is checked.

Leaks From Attic Equipment

In a lot of homes the attic houses an air handler along with its condensate line and pan. A clogged drain or an overflowing pan drips into the insulation for weeks, with no storm to warn you.

Bath Fans Venting Inside

Every day, a fan duct that stops short of the roof releases humid bathroom air into the attic. The condensation it leaves on the sheathing can pass for a roof leak.

What We Do

What Happens After You Call

1

Inspect and Find the Source

To learn whether you have a leak, condensation or the two together, we go over the roof deck, the vents, the exhaust ducts and each flashing point. Its full reach is mapped with a thermal camera and meter readings.

2

Take Out Wet Insulation

We bag and remove insulation that is moldy or saturated, which lets the drywall and wood beneath it dry. Rooms under the attic are protected, and the route in and out stays contained.

3

Dry the Framing

Air movers and dehumidifiers bring the rafters, joists and sheathing back to normal moisture levels. An antimicrobial goes on after any visible mold has been cleaned from the wood.

4

Correct the Source

We point out the roof or ventilation problem that needs correcting and can share our findings with your roofer. Once the attic stays dry, new insulation goes in to restore the R-value.
