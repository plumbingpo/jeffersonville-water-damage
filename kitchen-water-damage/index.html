Kitchen Water Damage in Jeffersonville

Count the dishwasher, disposal, drain, faucet and fridge line, and the kitchen has more places for water to connect than anywhere else in the house. Almost every one sits out of view, behind an appliance or inside a cabinet. When one drips, the water runs to the back of the cabinet base and spreads under the floor. We help Jeffersonville homeowners find it, dry it, and save as much of the kitchen as possible.

In a wet kitchen, the cabinets are where the real expense lies. Plywood and solid wood will often dry successfully, whereas a particleboard box puffs up quickly and never returns to size. By removing toe kicks and drilling small holes where they will not show, we push dry air beneath and behind the cabinets, and in many kitchens the countertops and cabinets never move.

Warning Signs

  • Along the cabinet line or beside the fridge, vinyl that feels soft or looks stained
  • A brown stain on the ceiling of the room below the kitchen
  • Doors on the cabinets that have gone out of alignment or started sticking
  • Wood or laminate flooring that cups or lifts in front of the dishwasher
  • Paint that bubbles, or a stain, on the wall at the back of the sink or dishwasher
  • Toe kicks that have swollen, darkened, or pulled away from the cabinet base
If Nothing Is Done

How the Damage Progresses

First hour

Water Runs Under

A failed fitting or hose sends water under appliances and to the rear of the cabinets. Through edges and seams, it works its way under the floor covering.

24 to 48 hours

Cabinets Start Swelling

Particleboard cabinet bases and toe kicks soak up water and swell. In the dark, enclosed gap below the cabinets, and at the back of the dishwasher, mold can start.

Day 3 to 7

Floors Begin Moving

Laminate swells at the edges, hardwood cups, and vinyl lifts once the adhesive under it fails. Cabinet doors and drawers drift out of line.

Past one week

Tear Out Likely

There is no restoring cabinet boxes once they have swelled. Mold at the back of the cabinets often forces their removal, countertop included, and the subfloor may be delaminating.

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Shut Off the Leak

First we close the line to the leaking appliance or fixture, slide out the dishwasher or fridge when needed, and identify the exact failed connection so the repair can happen ahead of drying.

2

Map the Moisture

We check the cabinet bases, the wall at their back and the floor throughout the room with thermal imaging and moisture meters. Under kitchen flooring, water often goes farther than anyone expects.

3

Dry Without Demolition

With the toe kicks off, air gets pushed below the cabinets and behind them. For several days the dehumidifiers and air movers keep running, and damp cavities receive an antimicrobial treatment.

4

Take Out Only What Failed

Cabinet sections, flooring or subfloor that cannot be saved are taken out carefully. After dry readings are confirmed, the appliances go back in and we help you arrange the remaining repairs.

Hidden Risks

What Hides Behind It

Sink Drain Leaks

Because it leaks just when the sink drains, a loose fitting or a worn disposal seal often goes unnoticed. A damp sink cabinet smells and grows mold quickly because that water has food waste in it.

Layers of Flooring

Kitchens often have new flooring laid over old. Water gets trapped between the layers, where it cannot evaporate and will not show up on the surface.

Toe Kick Space

Base cabinets sit over a gap that is boxed in all around. Water collects there, stays for weeks, and grows mold that nobody sees until the cabinets are pulled.

The Wall Behind the Cabinets

Supply and drain lines run through the wall behind the sink and dishwasher. When they leak, framing and drywall get wet behind the cabinets, and mold spreads where no one can see it.
