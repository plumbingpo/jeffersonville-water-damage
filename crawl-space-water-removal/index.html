Crawl Space Water Removal: What Jeffersonville Homeowners Should Know

Few homeowners notice right away when water collects beneath the floor. It tends to turn up as a damp chill underfoot, a stale smell at the vents, or a small lake discovered the day somebody lifts the hatch. Usually that means days of moisture in the joists already, plus insulation that is wet all the way through. Crews from our company extract the water, haul away anything that cannot be saved and dry the structure ahead of rot.

Once the standing water is gone, the job is only halfway done. Because lumber under a floor is slow to soak and slow to dry, our crew tracks its moisture content with a meter and runs dehumidification for the 3 to 5 days it normally takes to bring the readings down. Our team traces the source too, whether that is a supply line leak, a drain, or rain driven groundwater, so the space stays dry afterward.

What It Looks Like

  • Ducts and plumbing lines below the house covered in condensation
  • Corroded metal such as duct straps, pipe hangers and steel posts
  • Hardwood boards upstairs starting to cup, with edges higher than the centers
  • Dark water marks and chalky deposits partway up the block or concrete
  • An increase in insects and pests that like damp, dark spaces
  • Insulation that has pulled loose and hangs in wet clumps below the joists
Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

Settling Under the House

Long periods of standing water loosen the soil that holds up the support piers. You might notice it as a door that suddenly rubs or a floor that is no longer level.

Termites and Other Pests

Rodents, carpenter ants and termites all seek out moist lumber and soggy ground. Getting moisture levels down takes away a main reason for them to stay.

Wet Wiring and Ducts

Under a typical house you will find cable runs, electrical boxes and duct lines. When duct insulation is saturated it sends stale air around the home, while flooded wiring creates a real risk of shock.

Air From Below

Much of what you breathe on the main level drifts up from beneath the house, carrying any mold spores and musty smells along with it.

Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

First day

Puddles Form Underneath

Water collects in the low spots and soaks into the soil. Humidity in the space jumps within hours as the insulation pulls water into itself.

24 to 48 hours

Early Mold Growth

Growth may begin on wet framing and on the paper backing of the batts. Soaked insulation gains weight and starts to sag off the floor above.

Day 3 to 7

Dampness Rises Indoors

Damp air rises into the living space. The lumber goes on taking in moisture, a stale smell spreads and flooring can begin to cup.

After a week

Wood Starts Rotting

Decay begins in any framing that never got a chance to dry. Sill plates and joists lose firmness, nails and hangers corrode, and termites along with other pests move toward the moisture.

How We Fix It

How We Handle Crawl Space Water Removal

1

Inspect and Make Safe

Nobody enters until the space has been screened for shock risks, hanging ducts and contamination. Next comes charting the extent of the water and metering the joists and subfloor for moisture.

2

Pump Out Water

Standing water comes out with submersible pumps and hoses, down to the footing trenches and dips where it gathers. Any silt or mud sitting on the liner gets removed during this step.

3

Clear Out Ruined Material

Debris, saturated batts and any liner that cannot be reused leave the crawl space in bags. Where mold has begun or is likely, the bare joists and subfloor get cleaned and an antimicrobial applied.

4

Dry and Verify

A dehumidifier and low profile air movers stay running beneath the floor until the lumber meters at a normal level. We recheck with a meter and give you the readings and a prevention plan.
