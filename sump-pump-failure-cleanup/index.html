Sump Pump Failure Cleanup Help in Jeffersonville

A sump pump has one job, and it tends to fail at the moment it is needed most. Heavy rain raises the water table, the pit fills, and then the power goes out, the float sticks or the pump cannot keep up. Water rises over the rim of the pit and spreads across the basement floor. Until pumping resumes, the water continues to rise.

Clean is the wrong word for what comes out of a sump pit. It has come through soil and often carries silt, bacteria and whatever was in the drain tile, so we treat it as Category 2 at best and Category 3 if it has sat or mixed with sewage. Cleanup is different as a result, with wet drywall, carpet pad and other porous materials generally removed and the rest cleaned and disinfected ahead of drying.

First Five Minutes

Your First Steps

  1. Keep Out of the BasementNever wade into basement water before the power has been cut. Outlets, the pump cord or the furnace might already be wet and live.
  2. Turn Off the Basement CircuitsBasement breakers should be flipped off, provided you can get to the panel without standing in water. A panel surrounded by water is a job for the power company.
  3. Check the Pump SafelyA tripped outlet, a loose plug or a float wedged against the basin wall are the things to look for. Do not reach into the water.
  4. Assume the Water Is DirtyIf going down cannot be avoided, put on gloves and rubber boots, leave pets and children upstairs, and wash up after handling wet items.
  5. Save What Is Dry, Then CallGet dry valuables to a higher floor, snap photos showing the pump and how high the water is, and give us a call. Waiting for it to recede without help is a mistake.
Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Get the Water Out

Our crew arrives with extractors and pumps, which means your dead sump is not needed. Low spots, the area by the pit and the rest of the floor are cleared of water and silt.

2

Take Out What Got Wet

Our team wears protective gear to remove wet drywall, insulation, carpet and pad reached by groundwater, then bags and hauls it off, leaving bare framing and concrete.

3

Clean and Sanitize

The slab, wall framing and remaining contents are washed down and treated with an antimicrobial product. This happens before drying so contaminants are not blown around the basement.

4

Dry and Verify

We run drying equipment until our meters show normal moisture in the slab and the wood, which typically takes 3 to 5 days. Before we pull our equipment, the pit should have a pump that works.

Common Causes

Why This Happens

  • Outside the house, a crushed, clogged or separated discharge pipe.
  • Water returning down the discharge pipe because the check valve no longer seals.
  • Dirt and small rocks washed into the basin that lock up the pump.
  • A float switch that is stuck, tangled or pinned against the basin wall.
  • More water flowing into the pit than an undersized pump can move.
Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

Finished Basement Walls

Dirty water climbs from the slab into insulation and drywall, far higher than the line you can see. The lower part that got wet is cut away, not dried, on account of the contamination.

Basement Floor Coverings

There is no safe way to clean pad and carpet that sat in groundwater, so both are removed. Silty water also gets caught below vinyl plank and laminate, which normally means lifting them.

Boxes and Stored Items

Contaminated water is absorbed fast by fabrics, books, cardboard and the legs of furniture. Within days mold shows up on absorbent things that were in it, and they are difficult to disinfect.

Bottom Plates and Studs

Framing lumber at floor level takes on groundwater before anything else in the wall. We clean, disinfect and dry the studs and plates after removing the wet drywall.

Insurance

Insurance and Sump Pump Failure Cleanup

Sump pump losses are widely misunderstood when it comes to insurance. A standard homeowners policy usually excludes water that backs up or overflows from a sump, and flood insurance is a separate policy with its own rules. Unless you bought an endorsement for sump overflow or water backup there is often no coverage, and such endorsements are capped. Your declarations page will list it, or your agent can tell you. We record the pump failure, the water level and the damage so your claim file is complete.
